What you’re looking at

Tap Hold and speak — or any saved place — to see the whole thing. The driver never types an address in the normal case: the phone already knows where they are, and the destinations they use repeat.

The answer is deliberately not a map. It’s which run to take, what’s in the way, and what the camera actually shows — then a second option if the first one looks ugly.

The whole flow

  1. 01Phone reports where you are. No typing.
  2. 02You say or tap where you’re going.
  3. 03It checks Main Roads incidents against your likely routes.
  4. 04It reads the cameras along the way and describes them.
  5. 05It speaks the answer and shows it on screen.

Choices made here

Saved places beat a search box. Crew go to the same dozen venues. Typing an address one-handed at 6am is the thing to design out.

The camera is described, not just shown. A driver glancing at a phone shouldn’t have to interpret a picture.

Traffic-light colours. Green, amber, red. Nobody needs to be taught them.

Always dark. It gets used before sunrise in a truck cab, not at a desk.

What it deliberately won’t do

  • Nothing while moving. Built for use parked. Phones in hands while driving is illegal in WA, and this shouldn’t encourage it.
  • No turn-by-turn. It tells you which run to take, then you drive it however you normally would. It isn’t trying to replace your nav.
  • No background tracking. Closed or locked, a web app stops. It can’t follow anyone around.

Before this can be built

One question decides it: does Main Roads WA let us pull the camera images, and does the licence cover business use? If not, the camera panel goes and this becomes a much more ordinary app.

There’s a draft email in the repo asking exactly that.
